Explore DRC - Website Planning Document

WDD 131 Project Plan | Isaac Bolili Bakambala

1. Site Name

Explore DRC (exploredrc.org)

Reason for Selection: This name is short, memorable, and immediately communicates the site's mission—inviting both local Congolese visitors and international learners to discover the geography, major cities, demographics, languages, and rich cultural heritage of the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Explore DRC serves as an interactive cultural and demographic hub for the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The website provides structured profiles of major Congolese cities (such as Kinshasa, Lubumbashi, Goma, Mbuji-Mayi, and Kisangani), population insights, an overview of official and national languages (French, Lingala, Kikongo, Swahili, and Tshiluba), cultural traditions, and a visitor feedback/inquiry form.

3. Scenarios

  • Which major cities in the DRC have the largest populations, and what are their primary spoken languages?
  • What are the four national languages of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and in which regions are they spoken?
  • How can I filter Congolese cities by region or province to learn about their local culture and landmarks?

4. Color Schema

Adapted directly from the national DRC palette used in my Country/Place project:

Dark Blue: #003b70

Used for main headings (h1, h2), strong text, and the footer background.

DRC Blue: #007fff

Used for card section accents, buttons, and the first band of the tri-color flag border.

DRC Yellow: #f7d618

Used for accent borders, highlights, and the center band of the tri-color flag border.

DRC Red: #ce1021

Used sparingly for alerts, interactive hover states, and the final band of the tri-color border.

Neutrals: #f4f4f4 & #222

Light gray page background (#f4f4f4), white cards (#fff), and dark charcoal body text (#222).

5. Typography

  • Headings (Merriweather, serif): Used for the site title, hero banners, and section headings (h1, h2, h3) to give an authoritative, editorial feel.
  • Body & Navigation (Open Sans, sans-serif): Used for paragraphs, city data cards, tables, navigation links, and forms for clean legibility across mobile and desktop screens.
